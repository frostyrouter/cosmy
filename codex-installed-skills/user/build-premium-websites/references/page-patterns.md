## 1. Narrative architecture

Build the page as an argument, not a component catalog:

1. State the product promise.
2. Show the product or outcome.
3. Explain the most differentiating capabilities.
4. Prove the claim with evidence, demonstrations, or credible detail.
5. Resolve risk or uncertainty.
6. Present a clear next action.

For short campaigns, compress this to promise → proof → action. For complex products, create chapters separated by meaningful changes in scale, background, or pacing.

## 2. Hero patterns

Choose exactly one primary pattern:

### Product stage

Use centered text above or around a single product render. Keep supporting copy to 1–3 lines and actions to one primary plus one secondary. Allow the image to become the visual center of gravity.

### Editorial split

Use a 5/7 or 6/6 grid with left-aligned message and right-side image. Align the subject toward the copy. Stack copy before image on mobile unless immediate visual recognition is more important.

### Cinematic field

Use full-bleed photo/video with short high-contrast copy. Supply a stable poster, pause control for nonessential autoplay motion, and a contrast scrim. Avoid long paragraphs over media.

### Typographic manifesto

Use a large message as the visual. Apply only when the language is distinctive enough to deserve the space. Add a small visual proof immediately after the fold.

Hero defaults:

- Show the value proposition within the first viewport.
- Place the main CTA near the promise, not after a long preamble.
- Keep headline width between roughly 8 and 16 words per line depending on type size.
- Reveal no more than three layers of hierarchy: eyebrow, headline, support/action.
- Avoid carousels.

## 3. Proof and feature patterns

### Feature chapter

Use an eyebrow, a claim, one explanatory paragraph, and one dominant demonstration. Alternate composition only when it supports reading rhythm; do not mechanically zig-zag every row.

### Bento grid

Use only for genuinely parallel capabilities. Give one tile dominant area, one or two medium tiles, and supporting small tiles. Keep a shared baseline, radius, surface language, and image treatment. Collapse according to narrative priority, not source order.

### Metric band

Use 2–4 verified metrics with units, time frames, and comparison bases. Avoid fabricated counters and decorative numbers. Make the qualifier legible.

### Testimonial

Use a specific outcome or observation, real attribution, and permission-cleared portrait/logo. A single strong quote is more premium than a noisy rotating carousel.

### Comparison

Use when a decision depends on feature or tier differences. Keep row labels visible, support keyboard/screen-reader navigation, and offer a stacked mobile mode rather than horizontal microtext.

## 4. Product storytelling

- Demonstrate an interaction rather than describing it when the UI is the proof.
- Use scroll-linked sequences only when scrolling naturally explains progression, disassembly, transformation, or cause and effect.
- Keep explanatory text adjacent to the visual state it describes.
- Use sticky scenes for 1–3 major ideas, not an entire page.
- Provide static fallbacks for reduced motion, low power, and narrow viewports.
- Label technical diagrams precisely. Decorative lines must not imply false relationships.

## 5. Conversion endings

End with a decisive choice:

- Reiterate the core outcome in new, shorter language.
- Present one primary CTA and at most one lower-emphasis alternative.
- Address the last major objection nearby: price, compatibility, trial terms, availability, or contact route.
- Keep background contrast distinct enough to signal closure without introducing a new visual system.

## 6. Footer

- Use semantic navigation headings and real lists.
- Include only necessary product, company, resource, legal, locale, and social links.
- Keep 14px or larger body text when possible and preserve contrast.
- Collapse long link groups into accessible disclosure widgets on mobile if needed.
- Keep legal text visually quiet but readable.

## 7. Copy rules

- Put the customer outcome in the headline and the mechanism in the support copy.
- Prefer short Anglo-Saxon verbs and concrete nouns.
- Use sentence case by default.
- Keep eyebrows to 2–5 words, headlines to roughly 3–12 words, and feature descriptions to 1–3 sentences.
- Use numbers only when verifiable.
- Replace claims like “fast” with measured detail such as “exports a 4K draft in under two minutes” when evidence exists.
- Avoid copying reference-site phrasing. Draft original language tied to the actual product.

## 8. Anti-patterns

- A hero followed by six identical three-card rows.
- A “trusted by” strip containing invented logos.
- Scroll hijacking, mandatory intro animations, or hidden cursors.
- Parallax on body copy.
- Auto-advancing carousels.
- Decorative dashboards filled with fake data.
- Repeated centered headings that flatten the page rhythm.
- Arbitrary “Learn more” links with no destination context.
- A final CTA that introduces a new promise or audience.
