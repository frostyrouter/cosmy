## Start from meaning

Do not begin with components, animations, or references. Begin by inventorying the subject.

Extract:

- **Nouns:** objects, places, people, tools, artifacts, symbols.
- **Verbs:** actions users or the subject perform.
- **Materials:** paper, metal, glass, ink, fabric, pixels, sound, light.
- **Rituals:** browsing a shelf, opening a case, tuning an instrument, unfolding a map.
- **Tensions:** loud/quiet, public/private, precise/organic, permanent/ephemeral.
- **Sensory cues:** rhythm, weight, friction, temperature, density, echo.
- **User desire:** what the visitor should understand, feel, or do.

The strongest website ideas connect a meaningful noun to a meaningful verb. A music archive can invite visitors to pull, inspect, place, and play. An architecture portfolio might unfold, measure, layer, and reveal.

## Build the experience brief

Write this before layout work:

```text
Subject:
Audience:
Primary action:
Required content:
Emotional aftertaste:
Material world:
Core tension:
Visual thesis:
Interaction thesis:
Signature moment:
Constraints:
```

A visual thesis describes a world, not a collection of adjectives.

Weak: “Minimal, premium, cool, interactive.”

Strong: “A nocturnal listening archive where releases are handled like physical artifacts and the interface moves with the patience of a turntable.”

## Generate and select concepts

Produce at least three directions before committing. For each direction, define:

1. The metaphor.
2. The page behavior it creates.
3. The visual motif it creates.
4. The signature interaction it enables.
5. The risk or limitation.

Score each direction:

- **Relevant:** Does it come from the subject?
- **Generative:** Can it guide type, layout, copy, imagery, and motion?
- **Distinctive:** Would it still be recognizable without a logo?
- **Scalable:** Can it support every required section and device?
- **Usable:** Can users understand actions and content?
- **Feasible:** Can it be built and verified with the available stack and assets?

Choose the direction with the strongest system, not the loudest hero.

## Turn the concept into a system

Translate the chosen idea across layers:

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Typography | Is the voice archival, mechanical, intimate, editorial, raw, or precise? |
| Palette | What atmosphere and contrast belong to the subject? |
| Composition | Should the page feel shelved, staged, sequenced, scattered, measured, or cropped? |
| Geometry | Which shapes and edge treatments come from the material world? |
| Texture | What level of grain, pattern, reflection, or imperfection is meaningful? |
| Motion | How would the physical metaphor accelerate, settle, resist, or loop? |
| Copy | What vocabulary and sentence rhythm belong in this world? |

Use one dominant motif and one supporting motif. Repetition creates authorship; a new effect in every section creates noise.

## Shape the narrative

Think in beats:

1. **Arrival:** establish tone and identity.
2. **Orientation:** show what exists and what the visitor can do.
3. **Exploration:** let the visitor manipulate, browse, compare, or inspect.
4. **Payoff:** deliver the richest functional or emotional state.
5. **Close:** leave a concise next action or final thought.

Vary density across beats. Follow a dense visual moment with quiet space. Let one section be typographic, another object-led, and another interaction-led while preserving the same system.

## Write in the same voice

- Use vocabulary taken from the concept’s world.
- Prefer specific verbs over generic calls to action.
- Keep labels short enough to function as interface language.
- Avoid invented claims, fake testimonials, fake clients, and unsupported metrics.
- Mark placeholders clearly when real content is unavailable.
- Make empty, loading, playing, paused, and error copy feel like the same brand.

## Preserve originality

References are evidence, not templates. Study principles such as scale, rhythm, restraint, or choreography, then re-derive the result from the current subject.

Reject a direction when:

- it reproduces a recognizable hero, navigation, typography treatment, or signature animation;
- it depends on a proprietary asset or typeface;
- it could be described only through another company’s name;
- it uses fashionable effects with no relationship to content;
- it adds visual complexity without creating meaning or interaction value.
